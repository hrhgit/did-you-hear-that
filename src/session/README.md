# src/session

The visit as a state machine: entry, calibration, rooms, last door, replay, result and swapped repeat. It is the only part that knows the order of things. It reads time only through an injected timer and gets randomness from a seeded generator, so a whole visit can run instantly in a unit test.

May import from: `engine`, `audio/output`, `storage`, `copy`, `config`.

## Calibration and the listening page

- `calibration-controller.ts` plays the calibration sound while the calibration screen is open (`.kiro/specs/audible-start/design.md`, "The calibration music"). The visitor sets a piece of music to a comfortable listening volume: with the output shell's Calibration_Sound `music` it starts the shell's loop and hands it every target, and the played setting follows the shell's steps, at most 5 dB in 2 seconds. Where the music cannot be loaded the sound is the hiss, and it plays calibration repeats one after another, with the gap from `src/config` between the end of one and the start of the next. While the music is still loading, a start is remembered and the sound starts when loading ends, if the calibration is still open. Its snapshot gives the setting last played, which the visible control shows, the target, the sound, the top of the control for that sound and the line to show, from the copy of that sound. Any stop, a close and a keep end the sound, and the next calibration starts again at the bottom of the control.
- `listening-page-session.ts` holds the state and actions of the development listening page. It builds the Web Audio backend from the window it is given, the output shell and the calibration controller, and gives the page a snapshot: the output state, the calibration, the headphone check, one row per sound with its level and its last outcome, the range report and the clamp log. Its tests give it a fake backend in place of the window.

## The visit flow

The visit flow runs one visit at a time from the front door to the result: calibration, the headphone check, the rules, the rooms with a rest between them, the missed-sound replay, the result and the swapped repeat. The design is in `.kiro/specs/visit-flow/design.md`.

- **The contract.** `visit-flow-types.ts` holds every type the interface may call or read: the options, the flow's methods, the snapshot of each stage, the events, the visit log and the visit summary. It holds types only and runs nothing. Types that the engine and the output stage already define are re-exported, not copied. The interface is built by hand against this file, so a change to it only adds: new fields, stages, event kinds or methods. Nothing in it is removed, renamed or given a new meaning. `visit-flow-types.test.ts` checks at the type level that the logged ear and room results are the engine's own.
- **`create-visit-flow.ts`.** `createVisitFlow` is the only function the interface calls. It is created once for the page and holds one output shell and one calibration controller for the page's whole life. Each action that needs sound calls the shell's `activate` inside the call, so an action given from a key press or a click keeps the user gesture. The room snapshot holds nothing about the trial under way, and the only event about a trial, `trialScored`, is made in the same moment the trial is decided. Its `reaction` tells the interface what to show at once: a ripple for a caught sound, the house's line for an answer to silence, and nothing otherwise. Pauses, a hidden page, an interruption, a device change, the stop control and a left page follow the pause and stop tables of the design. A device change calibrates again and starts the interrupted room again. Time comes only from the injected timer and every random number from the seed. The test options `VisitFlowTestOptions` take a fake backend or an output shell and a hook, `onTrialPresented`, that tells a simulated visitor about each trial. The interface never gives them.
- **`room-runner.ts`.** `createRoomRunner` runs one attempt at a room: it waits the trial's gap, plays the sound or plays nothing for a no-sound trial, opens the response window at the moment the sound started on the page clock (or, for a no-sound trial, where a sound would have started), and decides the trial at the first answer in the window or with no response when the window ends. A pause discards a trial under way, and the same trial comes again after the pause. A sound the shell refuses is tried again after its gap, and three refusals in a row end the visit. `window-start.ts` and `answer-time.ts` hold the time arithmetic, and `visit-rooms.ts` gives the rooms of each visit type in order.
- **`practised-room-runner.ts`.** `createPractisedRoomRunner` puts practice before the room runner (audible-start, light plan of 2026-11-16). Each ear hears the room's sound at the anchor, or at the room's top where that is lower, the left and then the right; a side that misses it hears it once more, one upward step louder and never above the top (`practiceLevelsDb`). A side that still misses it pauses the room with `practiceNotHeard`: `resume` plays that side's practice again, and `goOnWithoutPractice` starts the counting. Practice sounds go out with the purpose `practice`, are scored as trials are, give `practiceSoundScored` with a ripple only for a catch, and are logged in `visitLog.practice`, never in the trials, the replay or the picture. `countingStarted` marks the start of the counted part. The swapped repeat has no practice.
- **`visit-log-recorder.ts`.** `createVisitLogRecorder` builds the visit log: every played trial once, in order, decided or discarded, with the calibrations, the headphone checks, the room attempts, the answers that decided nothing, the pauses and the refused plays. Times are milliseconds since the visit began. The log is frozen plain data that survives a JSON round trip, and it is the one record the replay and the result read.
- **The summary and the visit-level rule.** `summarize-visit.ts` turns a visit log into the visit summary: each floor's ear results, its no-sound trial and false alarm counts, the right-minus-left difference and whether the swapped repeat is offered. `judge-visit-false-alarms.ts` pools the false alarms over the no-sound trials of every finished room and says whether their rate is above the limit `visitFlowConfig.visitFalseAlarmRateUnreliableAboveFraction`. When it is, the summary marks every ear of the visit unreliable. That limit is set at 0.2, proposed by the visit-flow spec and decided by the owner's coordinator on 2026-11-16, at the owner's request; the owner may reverse it (question Q1 in the design). The rule only adds the reason `visitFalseAlarmRate`, and never removes a reason the search gave.

The flow draws nothing and stores nothing. Drawing and input are in `src/ui`. Saving a visit belongs to the result page, so the flow writes nothing to local storage.

## Helpers for the visit flow

- `room-search-cap.ts`: `roomSearchCapDb` rounds a room's highest playable level down to the threshold search's level grid, to give the search as its cap.
- `judge-headphone-check.ts`: `judgeHeadphoneCheck` turns the answer to the headphone check into `correct`, `swapped`, `bothSides` or `notHeard`.
- `ear-types-match.test.ts` checks that the output stage's `Ear` and the engine's `Ear` are the same type.

## The missed-sound replay

After the last room the house plays again the sounds the visitor did not catch, louder, from the room and the side they came from, in the order they came, and then leads on to the result. The design is in `.kiro/specs/missed-sound-replay/design.md`. The screen of the stage is built by hand in `src/ui`.

- **The contract's additions.** `visit-flow-types.ts` gained the method `startReplay`, the field `replay` of `MissedSoundReplaySnapshot`, which every snapshot of the stage holds, the event kind `replaySoundStarted`, and the replay's own types at the end of the file. Nothing in the file was removed, renamed or given a new meaning. The owner's test fixture in `src/ui` builds a snapshot of this stage as a literal; the owner chose option (a) of question Q6 and cast that literal before the required field was added. `visit-flow-types.test.ts` checks the additions at the type level, and `replay-types-match.test.ts` checks that the copy's names for sides, endings and pause reasons equal the session's.
- **The plan.** `missed-sound-replay-plan.ts` (`planMissedSoundReplay`) is a pure function of the visit log and the config. It holds every sound trial of a finished room run that went unanswered, once and in order, with its room, its ear and the level the output stage played it at, also when a limit lowered that level. It leaves out caught trials, discarded trials (the trial came again and is in the log once more), no-sound trials and the trials of a run that a device change abandoned. It also leaves out the answers on the wrong side; that reading of question Q3 was decided by the owner's coordinator on 2026-11-16, at the owner's request; the owner may reverse it.
- **The cap.** Each sound is asked for at the level it was missed at plus `replayRaiseDb` (20 dB, from brief 07), or at its cap when that is lower. The cap is the room's top in the last calibration of the log, held at most at the anchor cap: the cap of audio-safety rule 2 for that sound with the calibration the output stage holds. This reading of "the cap" is question Q5 and was decided by the owner's coordinator on 2026-11-16, at the owner's request; the owner may reverse it. The plan says of each sound whether the cap held it back, so its label can say so before it plays.
- **The selection.** When there are more missed sounds than `replaySoundsMaxCount`, `even-selection-indexes.ts` (`evenSelectionIndexes`) keeps that many, evenly spread over the visit's order, the first and the last included. The most count, 20, was decided by the owner's coordinator on 2026-11-16, at the owner's request; the owner may reverse it (Q1).
- **The counts.** `replay-counts.ts` (`replayCounts`) counts, on each side, the sound trials of finished room runs up to a trial order: made, caught, answered on the other side and missed, so that made is always the sum of the other three. While a sound plays the view counts the whole visit so far, through that sound's trial; this reading of question Q4 was decided by the owner's coordinator on 2026-11-16, at the owner's request; the owner may reverse it.
- **The render seed.** `replay-render-seed.ts` (`replayRenderSeed`) gives the seed of the replay's renders from the log alone: the draw of the visit's source that follows its render seed and one engine seed per room run. The flow draws that seed from the visit's source on entering the stage, and the two are equal, so a replay can be played again from a stored log.
- **The controller.** `create-missed-sound-replay.ts` (`createMissedSoundReplay`) runs one replay: its phase (`nothingMissed`, `announcing`, `playing` or `finished`), its one pending timer, its plays and the sound under way. It plays only through `output.play` with the Purpose `replay`, one sound at a time, each `replayGapMs` after the end of the one before; the gap, 400 ms, was decided by the owner's coordinator on 2026-11-16, at the owner's request; the owner may reverse it (Q2). A sound refused for an ordinary reason is asked for again one gap later, and `refusedPlaysInARowMaxCount` refusals in a row end the replay, not the visit. A first sound can be refused as busy when the last trial's sound still plays at the moment the stage is entered; it is then asked for again one gap later, and a busy refusal that comes before the first replayed sound has started does not count towards the refusals that end the replay. After the first sound has started, a busy refusal counts like any other. `replay-sound.ts` (`replaySoundOf`) gives a played sound as the view shows it, and `missed-sound-replay-view.ts` (`missedSoundReplayView`) is the pure view of one moment: every line and label the screen shows, the counts, and whether the replay can be started or skipped now.
- **The flow's part.** `create-visit-flow.ts` makes the controller when the last room ends, after `beginRoom`, and shows the stage. Nothing plays until the visitor presses start: `startReplay` calls `activate` within the call, as every action that may start sound does. `proceed` skips the replay, or goes on once it has finished; `stop` silences at once and shows the result. A hidden page or an interruption pauses the replay through the snapshot's `pause`, and `resume` plays the cut sound again from its start one gap later. A device change ends the replay, and a left page shows the result. Until the owner's screen calls `startReplay`, the stage plays nothing.
- **The log is not changed.** The replay reads the visit log and writes nothing to it, its pauses included. The result holds the same log object and the same summary whether the replay was watched, skipped, stopped or ended early.

## The result page

The result page draws the lit cross-section of the house from a pure model, and saves, lists and deletes visits through one session object. The design is in `.kiro/specs/result-page/design.md`. The page itself is built by hand in `src/ui`.

- **The contract.** `result-page-types.ts` holds every type the page may call or read for the result: the archive's options and methods, the saved visit, the list, the outcomes of saving and deleting, and the picture with its floors, wings and lines. It holds types only and runs nothing, and it imports only from `src/config` and the visit flow's contract. It declares its own `WallClock` and `StorageArea`, and `result-page-types-match.test.ts` checks at the type level that the wall clock of `src/config`, the storage area of `src/storage` and the copy's names for states and reasons fit them. `result-page-types.test.ts` checks it against the visit flow's contract and the browser's `Storage`. As with the visit flow's contract, a change to it only adds: nothing in it is removed, renamed or given a new meaning. Nothing was added to `visit-flow-types.ts`: a result snapshot already holds the visit log and the summary that a save needs, and `chooseSwappedRepeat` already opens the swapped repeat.
- **The archive.** `create-visit-archive.ts` (`createVisitArchive`) is created once for the page, beside the visit flow and independent of it, so the front door and the replay can use it too. It calls the function that gives local storage again inside a `try` at each method, never when it is created, and keeps no list between calls: `list` reads local storage at that moment. Saved visit ids are drawn from the seed it is given, dates come from the injected wall clock (`createSystemWallClock` of `src/config` on the page), and the list's order is the order of saving, newest first, so a system clock set back cannot reorder it. A visit is saved only when the page calls `save`, on the visitor's press. Saving the same visit log again on the same page changes only its headphone name, unless the visit now holds swapped repeats that its saved copy lacks (see "The swapped repeat" below). A save is refused when the tidy name is longer than `headphoneNameMaxCharacterCount` or as many visits are saved as `savedVisitsMaxCount`; both values, 40 and 20, were decided on 2026-11-15 on the owner's behalf, at the owner's request (questions Q5 and Q4), and a save at the limit is refused, never made room for by deleting a visit. Every refusal and every storage failure comes back as a reason with its line from `src/copy`, and no method throws because of anything local storage does or holds. `exportVisitLog` gives a listed visit's log as JSON text, for the no-headphone replay's data file.
- **Robot mode.** With the mode `robot` the archive never calls the function that gives local storage, not even to read. It lists the state `off` with no visits, refuses every save and delete with `robotMode`, and exports nothing, so the robot's runs neither keep anything nor show the owner's own saved visits.
- **The payload and the visit log.** `saved-visit-payload.ts` writes and reads what an entry holds beyond its envelope: format version 1, the `floorDb` and the swapped-repeat difference in force when it was saved, the summary exactly as the result showed it, and the visit log exactly as the flow recorded it. A saved visit's log read back, or exported, equals the JSON round trip of the log the result snapshot gave. The summary is not computed again from the log, so a later change of the summary code, or the owner's choice of the visit limit (0.2, visit-flow Q1, decided by the owner's coordinator on 2026-11-16, at the owner's request; the owner may reverse it), never redraws or rejects an old visit; the summary keeps the limit it was judged by. `parseSavedVisitPayload` gives null, never an exception, for a payload that is not JSON, of another version, or whose summary breaks one of the invariants `summarizeVisit` gives every summary. It checks the log only for its version, its visit type and its rooms; checking a log against the engine is the no-headphone replay's to do. The archive counts such an entry as unreadable and does not list it.
- **Headphone names.** `canonical-headphone-name.ts` gives the tidy form of a typed name (Unicode NFC, every run of white space or control characters made one space, trimmed, and null when nothing is left), and the key two names are matched by, which ignores letter case. The stored name keeps the visitor's letter case.
- **The picture model.** `result-picture.ts` (`resultPicture`) is a pure function from a live result or a saved visit to a frozen picture: one floor per room of the visit in visit order, which is the order of the house from the lowest floor up; the rooms the visit did not enter, drawn closed; for each wing its state, how far the light reaches, its line and, for an unreliable wing, the line for its first reason; for each floor the left and right difference as a short label and a sentence, and a description in words; the false alarm line, the notice when the whole visit is unreliable, the limits statement, and the caption and file name of the saved image. Every string comes from `src/copy`. Each floor is computed from its own room summary alone; the only fields made from more than one floor are the false alarm counts and the notice, which are about answers to silence. Only the live result offers the swapped repeat, because a saved visit's calibration is gone. A live picture uses `hearingMethodConfig.floorDb`, and a saved one the `floorDb` saved with it.
- **How far the light reaches.** `light-depth-fraction.ts` (`lightDepthFraction`) gives a wing's depth as a fraction of the wing, from 0 (dark) to 1 (the far wall), on its own floor's range: from the floor's own loudest playable level, its `searchCapDb`, down to the quietest playable level, `floorDb`. A threshold found at the floor's loudest lights 0.1 of the wing, one at the quietest 0.9, linear in decibels between them and held at the ends. A wing that caught the quietest sound the house plays reaches the far wall, a wing that did not catch the loudest stays dark, and an unreliable wing has no depth and is drawn hatched. The 1 and the 0 come from the brief. The 0.1 and the 0.9 were decided on 2026-11-15 on the owner's behalf, at the owner's request (Q1). On one floor a deeper wing caught a quieter sound; the same depth on two floors does not mean the same level. Whether five depths stacked one above the other still invite reading floors against each other was put as Q2, decided on 2026-11-15 on the owner's behalf, at the owner's request: the picture is kept, and its drawing carries no ticks, numbers or axis on a measuring line and nothing that joins one floor to another.
- **Comparison with an earlier visit.** `find-earlier-visit.ts` (`findEarlierVisit`) finds the visit to draw faintly behind the shown one: the newest listed visit saved before it whose headphone name is the same, ignoring letter case and spacing, or the newest of all for a visit not saved yet. A name left empty finds nothing, and a visit saved with no name is never found, because unnamed headphones may be different headphones. Given that visit, `resultPicture` gives every wing of a floor whose room the earlier visit also entered the earlier ear's state and an outline, placed with `lightDepthFraction` on the shown floor's own scale, so that in one wing the light and the outline both mean "deeper is quieter on this floor". An unreliable earlier ear has no outline, so an earlier visit that was unreliable as a whole has none. A floor the earlier visit did not enter has no outline. The picture's `comparison` names the earlier visit's date and says that each visit is relative to its own calibration; no sentence says that a floor got better or worse, because whether the calibration repeats well enough between visits is open. Without an earlier visit every `earlier` and `comparison` is null and the picture is as before.

## The swapped repeat

On a floor whose left and right were far apart, the house asks the visitor to put the left earpiece on the right ear and the right earpiece on the left, checks the swap with a hiss in one earpiece, runs the room again with the engine and the calibration of the first run, and says whether the difference seems to stay with the ears or to move with the headphones, or that the swap did not make it clear. The design is in `.kiro/specs/swapped-repeat/design.md`. The screen of the stage and the drawing of the comparison are built by hand in `src/ui`.

- **The contracts only grow.** `visit-flow-types.ts` gained the methods `confirmSwap`, `playSwapCheck`, `answerSwapCheck` and `startSwappedRoom`, the field `repeat` of `SwappedRepeatSnapshot`, the fields `swappedRepeats` and `turnBackLine` of `ResultSnapshot`, the event kind `swappedTrialScored`, one sentence in the comments of `answer` and `pause`, and the repeat's own types at the end of the file. `result-page-types.ts` gained the optional `VisitToSave.swappedRepeats`, `SavedVisit.swappedRepeats`, `PictureFloor.repeat`, the method `VisitArchive.savedCopyLacksRepeats` and one sentence in the comment of `save`. Nothing in either file was removed, renamed or given a new meaning, apart from what `save` does for a saved copy that lacks repeats, a case that did not exist before (Q5). Three test literals in the owner's files under `src/ui` build the grown types; the owner's coordinator cast them before the fields were added, so they compile before and after (Q8, chosen by the owner's coordinator on 2026-11-15; the owner may reverse it). `visit-flow-types.test.ts` and `result-page-types.test.ts` check the additions at the type level, and `swapped-repeat-types-match.test.ts` checks that the copy's names for sides, outcomes, reasons, endings, pause reasons, arrangements and earpieces equal the session's.
- **Ears and earpieces.** An ear is the visitor's own ear, the side the visitor names in an answer. An earpiece is a side of the headphones, the channel the output stage fills; the output's `ear` field names an earpiece. `earpiece-on-ear.ts` (`earpieceOnEar`) gives the earpiece on each ear as marked and swapped. The search of the repeat runs over ears exactly as in every room, and `swapped-earpiece-output.ts` (`swappedEarpieceOutput`) is the output as the repeat's room runner sees it: every `play` goes to the earpiece on its ear, and nothing else changes.
- **The record.** `swapped-repeat-run.ts` (`swappedRepeatRunOf`) turns the run's own recorder log into a `SwappedRepeatRun`: every trial with its ear and its earpiece, the earpiece on the side named for each answer that decided a trial and for each answer that decided nothing, every swap check with the earpiece it played in, and which earpiece sat on which ear. Its ears are judged by the helpers `summarize-visit.ts` now exports unchanged, so a sound played at another level than asked makes an ear unreliable with `levelNotAsAsked`, as in a room. The record is frozen plain data that survives a JSON round trip, and its times are on the visit log's clock.
- **The earpiece state.** The flow keeps what it knows of how the headphones sit: `asMarked` after a visit's headphone check answered `correct`, `unknown` from the moment the stage shows the instruction, `swapped` after a swap check answered on the right, and `unknown` again after any other answer and whenever the stage is left after the instruction. No room run starts unless the state is `swapped`. The phase `finished` gives the line that asks the visitor to turn the headphones back, and so does every result while the state is not `asMarked`. One line and no check of the turn back is Q7, decided by the owner's coordinator on 2026-11-16, at the owner's request; the owner may reverse it.
- **The swap check.** `judge-swap-check.ts` (`judgeSwapCheck`) judges the answer to "Which side was the hiss on?": `right` is `confirmed`, `left` is `notSwapped` and shows the instruction again, and both sides and nothing heard keep the visitor in the check with the visit's own lines. The hiss is the visit's own, `playHeadphoneCheck` in the left earpiece (Q3, decided by the owner's coordinator on 2026-11-16, at the owner's request; the owner may reverse it). The output stage's rise limit may play it quieter than asked after a room or a replay, and it comes up one step a press (Q11, decided by the owner's coordinator on 2026-11-16, at the owner's request; the owner may reverse it).
- **The outcome.** `swapped-repeat-outcome.ts` (`swappedRepeatOutcome`) is the brief's rule, kept as it is (Q1, decided by the owner on 2026-11-15): `unclear` with `noLevel` when an ear of the repeat has no level, `unclear` with `differenceShrank` when the repeat's left and right came out less than half the limit apart, `followsEar` when the same ear needed the louder sound both times, and `followsEarpiece` when the same earpiece did. It corrects no number of the first run.
- **The engine seed.** `swapped-repeat-engine-seed.ts` (`swappedRepeatEngineSeed`) gives a run's engine seed from the visit log and the run number alone: the draw of the visit's source after the render seed, the rooms' engine seeds, the replay's render seed and the seeds of the earlier repeat runs. The flow draws the same seed from the visit's source when the room starts, and a run that did not finish still counts.
- **The controller.** `create-swapped-repeat.ts` (`createSwappedRepeat`) runs one visit to the stage: the phases `unavailable`, `instruction`, `check`, `ready`, `room` and `finished`, the swap checks, the room run on its own recorder and the view. It plays only the hiss and the room's trials, calls `beginRoom` after the last check and before the first trial, pauses only in the room, retries a refused trial as the room runner does, and ends the repeat, not the visit, after three refusals in a row. A floor whose calibration is gone, or was measured with an earlier calibration, opens `unavailable` and plays nothing (Q6, decided by the owner's coordinator on 2026-11-16, at the owner's request; the owner may reverse it). A run that does not reach its end gives no record and is shown nowhere (Q10, decided by the owner's coordinator on 2026-11-16, at the owner's request; the owner may reverse it). `swapped-repeat-view.ts` (`swappedRepeatView`) is the pure view of one moment, with every line and label the screen shows, and in the room it does not change while a trial waits for its answer. `swapped-repeat-comparison.ts` (`swappedRepeatComparison`) sets the first run and the repeat side by side, by ear and each labelled with its earpiece, on the floor's own scale, with the outcome in words.
- **The flow's part.** `create-visit-flow.ts` opens the stage from the result only for a floor that offers the repeat and has no record yet (Q4, decided by the owner on 2026-11-15: other floors that offer it still do). `playSwapCheck`, `startSwappedRoom` and `resume` call `activate` within the call, as every press that may start sound does; nothing plays on entering the stage or confirming the swap. `proceed` goes back to the picture and `stop` silences at once and goes back, both ending a run under way without a record. Pauses of the stage are written to the run's record, never to the visit log, and a dropped visit ends the repeat too. The test hook gives each presented trial its earpiece and, in a repeat, its run number.
- **The visit log and the summary are not changed.** The repeat writes nothing to the visit log, and the result after it holds the same log object and the same summary object as before it.
- **The picture.** `result-picture.ts` gives each floor with a record its comparison in `repeat`, and no offer of the repeat there; every other field of the floor and of the picture is what it is without repeats.
- **Saved visits.** `saved-visit-payload.ts` writes the records under the key `swappedRepeats`, after the visit log, only when the visit holds at least one; a visit without one is written byte for byte as before, and the format version stays 1. A payload without the key reads with no records. A payload with it is readable only when `swapped-repeat-run-holds.ts` (`swappedRepeatRunsHold`) finds every record sound, its outcome worked out again from its own numbers included; otherwise the whole entry is unreadable.
- **A visit saved before its repeat.** Saving again a visit this page saved, whose saved copy is still listed and lacks records the visit now holds, replaces the stored payload with `changePayload` of the visit store and keeps the saved visit's id, date and name, with the line `savedAgainLine` (Q5, decided by the owner on 2026-11-15). `savedCopyLacksRepeats` tells the page when the saved copy lags, so that the page shows its save control again and compares nothing itself (the sub-question of Q5, chosen by the owner's coordinator on 2026-11-15; the owner may reverse it). It is false for a visit this page has not saved, in robot mode and whenever local storage cannot be read, and it never throws.
