# Product

**Did You Hear That?** is a haunted house for headphones that runs in a browser. Something in the house makes a sound on the visitor's left or right. Each time the visitor catches it, the next sound is 10 dB quieter. Each time they miss it, the next sound is 5 dB louder. The house is running a hearing threshold search and the visitor experiences it as a monster that keeps getting quieter.

It is an entry for Kiroween 2026 in the Costume Contest category. That category asks for a haunting interface whose spooky elements improve the app's function. Here the horror rule is the measurement procedure, so every design choice must serve both.

## Who it is for

People who wear headphones for hours a day and have never had their hearing checked, because the usual check is a row of beeps nobody volunteers for. The visitor is alone, on a laptop or a phone, with whatever headphones they own.

## What the visitor does

1. Enters the house. Chooses the visit of about 7 minutes, the visit of about 12 minutes, or a 60 second replay that needs no headphones.
2. Calibrates. Sets a piece of music in the headphones to the volume at which they would listen to music in a quiet room, clear and comfortable, without changing the system volume. Confirms left and right are not swapped.
3. Enters one dark room, carrying a lantern. The creatures of the house live there, one for each pitch of the visit: three in the visit of about 7 minutes, five in the other. First the roll call: each creature makes its sound once in each ear, loud enough to hear easily. Then it counts: the creatures take turns in no order, each on the left or the right, and each time one is caught it comes back quieter, until its search is over and it goes home. On some turns the house makes no sound at all. The lantern shows how the chase goes: a catch lights one of its three lights, an answer on the wrong side or at nothing dims one, and a dimmed light returns by itself after a while. A dark lantern stops nothing: the room goes on and every answer counts (the owner, 2026-11-17, after finding a room that would not answer too frustrating). When the room has been quiet for about 12 seconds with no catch, the creature whose turn it is makes its sound once, louder (a reminder, which counts for nothing in the search), and each catch is followed by a soft chime.
4. Opens the last door (the visit of about 12 minutes only). A voice says three digits in noise and the visitor types them.
5. Hears the replay. Every sound they missed is played again, louder, from the side where it was, with its creature.
6. Sees the result. A cross-section of a five-storey house with a left wing and a right wing. Light reaches as deep into each wing as that ear could hear on that floor.
7. Swaps the headphones and repeats a floor where left and right differed a lot. Each such floor can be repeated until one run of it ends with a record.

## What the result may claim

Consumer headphones are not calibrated and differ from each other at every pitch. Two comparisons survive that:

- the left ear against the right ear on the same floor, checked by the swapped-headphone repeat
- the same person with the same headphones, this visit against a later visit

Nothing else is claimed. Floors are never compared with each other. There is no comparison with other people, no clinical scale and no verdict.

## Experience rules

- The fear comes from things getting quieter. There are no sudden loud sounds anywhere in the product.
- Before the visitor answers, nothing on screen may reveal when a sound plays or which side it is on. The one exception is the practice at the start of the room, the roll call: while a practice sound plays, the screen may show which creature makes it, in the middle of the room, and say that a sound is playing now, never which side. Practice counts for nothing, comes before every counted sound of the room, and is there so that the visitor knows the house, the creatures and the headphones work and what to do. Once the counting begins, the screen never shows whose turn it is.
- After a trial is scored, and not before, the screen may say what happened: a caught sound shows its creature in the doorway of its side, and a sound that went by is shown leaving by its side ("Scritch went by on your left"). A creature whose search is over goes home, which follows its last scored trial (`.kiro/specs/one-room/design.md`, decided by the owner's coordinator on 2026-11-16 at the owner's request). Its time to answer is over by then, and the next trial's side is drawn afresh. The lantern may change between trials, because it follows only the visitor's own answers, a catch at the moment it is scored, and the clock (the game layer, decided by the owner on 2026-11-16). A reminder sound is scored like a trial and may be drawn like one once it is: a caught reminder shows its creature on its side. A streak of catches is counted on screen, and an unanswered turn with no sound says so once it is over ("Nothing came that time.").
- A visitor with no headphones, and a judge who only watches a video, must still be able to follow what happened. Every caught sound is drawn as its creature with rings and a line naming its pitch and level, and the replay mode shows a whole recorded visit.
- All interface text is in English.

## What it is not

- Not a medical device, not a diagnosis, not a validated screening test.
- No accounts, no server, no analytics. Results stay in the visitor's browser.
- No leaderboard and no comparison between visitors.
- No correction for specific headphone models.
