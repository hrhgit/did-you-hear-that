# tests/e2e

Playwright tests, named `*.spec.ts`. `npm run test:e2e` builds the site, serves `dist/` locally and runs them at a desktop size and a phone size. Vitest does not collect files from this directory. `preview-servers.ts` lists the two addresses the built page is served at, the server root and `/did-you-hear-that/`, and both `playwright.config.ts` and the tests read it.

`listening-page.spec.ts` checks the development listening page of the audio-output spec in headless Chromium. The build does not hold that page, so `playwright.config.ts` also starts the development server at the port in `dev-server.ts`, for this file and for the two development sheets below. The tests install probes before the page's scripts that keep a copy of every buffer the page hands to Web Audio, render the output graph in an `OfflineAudioContext`, and check that the built `dist/` holds none of the page's strings. The browser runs muted.

`front-door.spec.ts` checks the page a visitor lands on, at both addresses: the title, the line about headphones, the three ways in, the door that is not open yet, the dark background and the contrast of its text, that nothing is ready to sound before a press, that nothing scrolls sideways down to 360 by 640, keyboard focus, and the accessibility rules of axe-core.

`visit.spec.ts` walks a visit in the built page: from the front door through the headphone check and the rules into the one room. It checks when the theme starts and stops on entry and at the result. It answers the roll call by keyboard at the desktop size and by touch at the phone size until the house says who it was, checks that the roll is called before anything counts and that the game starts automatically even when a practice sound was missed, pauses and goes on, stops and comes back to the front door. The browser runs muted, so these tests check what the page shows and that its controls work. They do not check what anything sounds like.

`room.spec.ts` checks the one room on the development replay sheet, which is the page of a visit with no visit flow and no sound. `simulated-room.ts` plays whole visits in Node with the real visit flow and a simulated visitor, with the searches taking turns as the shipped site runs them, and gives every snapshot and every event in the order a page is handed them. The tests hand those moments to the sheet and check what the room draws for each: a creature in the doorway of the side its practice sound plays from while that sound plays, and in the middle of the room between sounds, with the line naming no side; rings, a hop and the creature's name for a caught practice sound; a lit lantern and counting cards when it begins to count; the creature of a caught sound in the doorway of its side and on its card; a sound that went by leaving through its doorway; the line for the other side and the fixed line for an answer to no sound; only the line that nothing came, with nothing drawn in the room, when a turn with no sound goes by unanswered; the slow dimming of a dark lantern; a creature going home; the choice after a practice with no catch; and a pause. They check each of these with every motion and with less motion, and that every change of brightness is a fade. At seven sizes of screen, from 360 by 640 to 1280 by 800 and on a phone on its side, they check that no part of the room lies over another, that every part is on the page, that no word on a card is cut off, and that nothing scrolls sideways: at the roll call with the longest introduction, while it counts just after a creature went home, and with a dark lantern and long counts. These layouts are measured with less motion, so that the swaying lantern is read at rest. The browser runs muted.

`result-page.spec.ts` checks the result page on the development server, on visits that `simulated-result.ts` plays in Node with the real visit flow and a simulated visitor, in the one room. It checks that each state of a wing is drawn its own way, that the statement of what the picture can tell stands next to the drawing in full at both sizes, that nothing overlaps with a headphone name of the greatest length, that a visit is saved only on a press and can be shown, renamed and deleted, that typing the name of the headphones of an earlier saved visit draws that visit as outlines, that the saved image is a PNG of the expected width, that reduced motion turns the spreading light into a short fade, and that the built `dist/` does not hold the development page. The browser runs muted.

`replay.spec.ts` checks the replay of missed sounds on the development server, on the development replay sheet, which is the page of a visit with no visit flow and no sound. `simulated-replay.ts` plays a visit in Node and lays out every moment of its replay with the earlier replay functions of `src/session`. The tests check that nothing can be started by a press made in the instant the first sheet appears, that the stop control is in view at every moment, that each sound shows its creature in the doorway of its side, its place, its words and the count of each side, that the words stay apart from one another and from the creature down to 360 by 640 and on a screen on its side, that the creature and the rings of a sound are drawn at the doorway of the side it came from, with every motion and with less motion, that a paused replay shows its own line and a way on, what the page says after the last sound and when no sound was missed, and that the built `dist/` does not hold the development page. They do not start the sounds of a replay: the development sheet has none. The browser runs muted.

`swapped-repeat.spec.ts` checks the swapped repeat of a floor on the same development sheet. `swapped-repeat-fixtures.ts` holds one snapshot, written out by hand, for every moment of a repeat that the page draws differently. The tests check what each step shows and asks the flow for: the sheet that asks for the swap, the check, whose answers count only after a check has played, the room, which is lit, answered and paused like the one room of the visit, both runs side by side for each of the three outcomes, and a repeat that was stopped or cannot start. They also check that the stop control is in view at every step, that the way back takes no press made in the instant the two runs appear, where the keyboard focus goes, that nothing scrolls sideways, and the accessibility rules of axe-core. They do not check what the visit flow does. The property tests and the simulated visits in `tests/properties` do that. The browser runs muted and the development sheet has no sound.

`result-repeat.spec.ts` checks the result page after a real swapped repeat. `simulated-result.ts` plays a visit in Node with the real visit flow, then has the simulated visitor repeat one floor with the headphones swapped, answer the check and the room through what it wears, and go back to the result. Two visitors are played: one whose right ear needs louder sounds, with even headphones, and one with even ears whose right earpiece plays 25 dB quieter. The tests check that the line asking to turn the headphones back is said, that the two runs stand beside the floor visited again with the outcome the session gives (the louder sound followed the ear for the first visitor and the earpiece for the second), that this floor no longer offers the repeat, that a visit saved before the repeat says its saved copy lacks it until it is saved again, and that the copy saved again draws the repeat after the page is opened again. They also check that nothing scrolls sideways and the accessibility rules of axe-core. The browser runs muted.

`whole-visit.spec.ts` walks one unanswered visit in the built page, from roll call to the automatic result. Run it with `WHOLE_VISIT=1 npx playwright test whole-visit --project=desktop`.

`whole-swapped-repeat.spec.ts` walks one whole visit in the built page and then visits a floor again with the headphones swapped, from the result page to the comparison and back. A listener installed in the page before its scripts reads every sound the page starts, its channel and its peak, and presses the key of the ear that would hear it, the way a visitor with a quieter left ear would. It swaps its ears when the sheet asks for the swap. It is skipped unless it is asked for: `WHOLE_VISIT=1 npx playwright test whole-swapped-repeat --project=desktop`. It checks that the swapped repeat runs from end to end with the real visit flow and that the result shows it beside its floor, in a muted browser, not what anything sounds like.

`page-checks.ts` holds what these files share: opening a page with probes that count the page's sound sources and with a record of requests to other origins and of uncaught errors, reading those counts, waiting until the page has come to rest (every fade and every motion that ends; what loops, as the lantern's sway does, is left out), the contrast arithmetic, the list of boxes that lie over one another, the check for sideways scrolling and the accessibility rules of axe-core.
