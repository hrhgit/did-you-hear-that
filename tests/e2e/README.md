# tests/e2e

Playwright tests, named `*.spec.ts`. `npm run test:e2e` builds the site, serves `dist/` locally and runs them at a desktop size and a phone size. Vitest does not collect files from this directory. `preview-servers.ts` lists the two addresses the built page is served at, the server root and `/did-you-hear-that/`, and both `playwright.config.ts` and the tests read it.

`listening-page.spec.ts` checks the development listening page of the audio-output spec in headless Chromium. The build does not hold that page, so `playwright.config.ts` also starts the development server at the port in `dev-server.ts`, for this file and for the two development sheets below. The tests install probes before the page's scripts that keep a copy of every buffer the page hands to Web Audio, render the output graph in an `OfflineAudioContext`, and check that the built `dist/` holds none of the page's strings. The browser runs muted.

`front-door.spec.ts` checks the page a visitor lands on, at both addresses: the title, the line about headphones, the three ways in, the door that is not open yet, the dark background and the contrast of its text, that nothing is ready to sound before a press, that nothing scrolls sideways down to 360 by 640, keyboard focus, and the accessibility rules of axe-core.

`visit.spec.ts` walks a visit in the built page: from the front door through the calibration, the headphone check and the rules into the first room, answers by keyboard at the desktop size and by touch at the phone size until the house says something, pauses and goes on, stops and comes back to the front door. The browser runs muted, so these tests check what the page shows and that its controls work. They do not check what anything sounds like.

`result-page.spec.ts` checks the result page on the development server, on visits that `simulated-result.ts` plays in Node with the real visit flow and a simulated visitor. It checks that each state of a wing is drawn its own way, that the statement of what the picture can tell stands next to the drawing in full at both sizes, that nothing overlaps with a headphone name of the greatest length, that a visit is saved only on a press and can be shown, renamed and deleted, that typing the name of the headphones of an earlier saved visit draws that visit as outlines, that the saved image is a PNG of the expected width, that reduced motion turns the spreading light into a short fade, and that the built `dist/` does not hold the development page. The browser runs muted.

`replay.spec.ts` checks the replay of missed sounds on the development server, on the development replay sheet, which is the page of a visit with no visit flow and no sound. `simulated-replay.ts` plays a visit in Node and lays out every moment of its replay with the same functions of `src/session` that the visit flow uses. The tests check that nothing can be started by a press made in the instant the first sheet appears, that the stop control is in view at every moment, that each sound lights its room and shows its place, its words and the count of each side, that nothing overlaps down to 360 by 640 and on a screen on its side, that the ripple of a sound is drawn on the side it came from, that a paused replay shows its own line and a way on, what the page says after the last sound and when no sound was missed, and that the built `dist/` does not hold the development page. They do not start the sounds of a replay: the development sheet has none. The browser runs muted.

`whole-visit.spec.ts` walks one whole short visit in the built page with nobody answering: every room, the replay of the missed sounds from its first sound to its last, and the picture. It takes several minutes of real time, because the page waits for answers that never come, so it is skipped unless it is asked for: `WHOLE_VISIT=1 npx playwright test whole-visit --project=desktop`. It checks that the page gets through the replay and what it shows on the way, in a muted browser. `visit-steps.ts` holds the steps from the front door into the first room, which this file and `visit.spec.ts` share.

`page-checks.ts` holds what these files share: opening a page with probes that count the page's sound sources and with a record of requests to other origins and of uncaught errors, the contrast arithmetic and the check for sideways scrolling.
